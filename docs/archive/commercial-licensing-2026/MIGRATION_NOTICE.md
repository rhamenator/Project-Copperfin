## What This Does *Not* Change

GPL-3.0 grants, once given, cannot be revoked for the versions they were
granted for. Specifically:

- Anyone who obtained a Legacy Version under GPL-3.0 — including the
  commercial entities that have already forked the GPL-licensed
  codebase — **keeps their GPL-3.0 rights to that Legacy Version forever**.
  They may continue to use, modify, and redistribute that version,
  including for commercial purposes, exactly as GPL-3.0 has always
  permitted. This relicensing does **not** retroactively restrict any
  fork or use of code already released under GPL-3.0.
- Those existing forks simply do not automatically receive new
  improvements released after the Effective Date under the new dual
  license — to get those, they (or anyone) would need to comply with the
  Source-Available License or obtain a Commercial License, the same as any
  other user.
- No GPL-licensed code already distributed is being "taken back." The
  license change applies only to code first released after the Effective
  Date.
